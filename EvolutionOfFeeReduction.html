Dual pricing and surcharging weren't always legal.

Both programs exist today because of a specific chain of federal law, antitrust litigation, and a Supreme Court ruling. Watch what happens with each new step up.

Before 1980

Card networks simply ban surcharging.

The 1980s

States write the ban into law too.

2010–11

Durbin protects the cash discount nationwide.

Jan 2013

Visa & Mastercard drop their own ban.

2013–15

Courts strike down state bans as unconstitutional.

2017

The Supreme Court rules it's a speech issue.

Today

A patchwork by state, and a safer nationwide default.

Worth Noting

Gas stations have posted a cash price and a credit price side by side for decades, long before any of this became common elsewhere. One product, one sign, made them the earliest adopters of dual pricing, and still the easiest real-world example to point to when a merchant asks what this actually looks like.

Compliance Caution

Card brand rate caps and state law are not the same thing, and both change over time. Never quote a specific surcharge cap or represent a state as "cleared" from memory alone, always confirm current requirements with Propelr Compliance or Legal before a program goes live.

Reflect

Two moments created the landscape you're selling into: a 2010 law that made cash discounting safe everywhere, and a 2013 settlement that made surcharging possible almost everywhere. When you're not sure which state you're standing in, lead with the one that doesn't have an "almost" attached to it.
